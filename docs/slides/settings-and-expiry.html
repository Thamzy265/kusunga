Settings and expiry

All settings in one place, old history removed after a time you choose, and Kusunga starting when you log in.

Use the arrow keys to move between slides · Print to save as PDF

Problem and users

History only goes when it is pushed out by newer copies

Who

Anyone who uses Kusunga every day.

The job they are doing

Keeping recent copies handy without keeping last month's copies for ever, and having Kusunga ready after a restart.

Today's pain

A copy stays until 200 newer copies push it out, which can take weeks. Kusunga has to be opened by hand after every login. Settings are spread over three tabs.

Success measure

Nothing older than the chosen time is shown or kept. A shorter time always says how many items will go. The login switch always matches the OS.

Plan item M1.7 (docs/PLAN.md). Resized from small to large: it changes how long personal data is kept and adds login items on two OSes. Details: docs/design/settings-and-expiry.md, docs/ui/settings-and-expiry.md.

Scope for this phase

Two new settings, and one Settings tab for all of them

In this phase

  • Auto-delete: Never, after 1 day, 7 days or 30 days. Snippets are never removed
  • Launch at login, off until you turn it on, starting in the menu bar or tray
  • A Settings tab with the history limit, the picker shortcut, auto-delete and launch at login
  • A link to Privacy for the ignore list and pause

Deferred (Later lines with triggers)

  • Auto-delete after 1 hour, trigger: ≥ 5 beta requests (Pause covers short moments)
  • Auto-delete per app, trigger: ≥ 5 beta requests
  • Export and import of settings: already planned in M1.8

How it works

Hidden at once, deleted soon after

1 Expired items are left out of History and the picker straight away→ 2 A clean-up deletes them at launch, every 15 minutes and on wake→ 3 Deleted text is overwritten in the encrypted file

Login on Mac

The built-in macOS login item (macOS 13 and later). It works inside the sandbox and needs no helper app. If macOS asks you to allow it, Kusunga shows a button to the right System Settings page.

Login on Windows

The usual per-user start-up entry, with no admin rights. If you turn it off in Task Manager, Kusunga shows that.

The OS holds the login setting, not Kusunga, so the switch can't drift from what really happens.

Plan and how you'll check it

Built in three steps

1 Auto-delete setting and clean-up→ 2 Launch at login on Mac, then Windows→ 3 Settings tab

How we will check it

  • Automated: tests for the clean-up clock (including a clock change and a wake), the upgrade default, the confirm count and the login bridges
  • QA on your Mac in the test profile: auto-delete removes old test items, the Settings tab, and the login switch
  • What only you can check: a real log-out and log-in, and any macOS "allow" prompt. Also VoiceOver

Decisions needed

5 decisions for you

1. Where do settings live?

Options: A, a new Settings tab, with the limit and the picker shortcut moved there; or B, leave those in History and add a small Settings tab for the new ones

Recommendation: A, so every setting is in one place

2. Which time starts the clock?

Options: A, the last time you copied the item; or B, the first time

Recommendation: A, because something you keep copying is something you still use

3. Default auto-delete

Options: A, Never for everyone; B, 30 days for new installs and Never for existing history; or C, 7 days for new installs and Never for existing history

Recommendation: B: private by default for new users, and nobody's history is deleted by an upgrade

4. Login on macOS 12

Options: A, raise the minimum to macOS 13; or B, show the switch disabled on macOS 12 with "Needs macOS 13 or later"

Recommendation: B, which cuts nobody off now. Revisit at M1.9

5. Open the window at login?

Options: A, no, start in the menu bar or tray only; or B, open the window as today

Recommendation: A, so a login start doesn't put a window in front of you

Assumed yes unless you say otherwise: lawful basis n/a while nothing leaves the device (as plan D7); no ADR, because none of these choices is hard to reverse; M1.7 resized to large.
